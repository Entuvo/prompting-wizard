# Day 11 — Numeral: checkable budgets

## Concept

A useful quantity tells you what to count and why the limit matters. "Some options for {{TASK}}" leaves both count and length open. "Three alternative subject lines, each under 40 characters" makes a real formatting requirement checkable.

Exact counts suit tasks that genuinely require that many items. Evidence-driven tasks need room for fewer: "Up to five supported risks, each under 40 words; return fewer if the evidence supports fewer." Demanding exactly five risks can encourage invented ones.

A ceiling or range is precise when its boundaries and units are clear. "Under 40 words" is checkable; "fairly short" is not. More numbers do not automatically make a prompt better: bound only quantities that affect success.

Here is the test: can you check each necessary limit, and could satisfying it force the model to invent content? Choose exact counts, ranges, or ceilings according to the task.

## Before / After

**Before**

> Give me some options for {{TASK}}.

**After**

> Give me up to three viable options for {{TASK}}, each under 40 words, ranked by cost. Return fewer if fewer are supported.

Count and length are checkable. The ceiling limits reading effort while allowing fewer viable options. Contrast an exact-count formatting task: "Write exactly three alternative subject lines, each under 40 characters."

## Exercise

Pick one of your recurring tasks. Write a single prompt for it.

### Novice

Fill the blanks with how many you want and how long each should be — a number for the count, and words rather than a number for the length ("fairly short", "a paragraph or so") — then send the completed line as your prompt.

> Give me ________ options for {{TASK}}, each ________.

### Working

Write a prompt for {{TASK}} that puts checkable limits on the quantities that matter. Explain why the count should be exact, a range, or a ceiling.

### Advanced

Write a prompt for {{TASK}} with task-appropriate, checkable limits. If content depends on evidence, allow fewer items rather than invention. If the task genuinely requires an exact count, preserve it and explain why.
