# Day 19 — Reasoning scaffolds: name the intermediates

## Concept

"Think step by step about {{TASK}}" asks for reasoning without saying what the steps are. The model produces steps — some invented, in whatever order looks plausible — and you can't tell whether the ones that mattered actually appeared, because you never named them.

A scaffold names the specific intermediates the task depends on, not generic "steps." For a scheduling conflict, that's "list each commitment's hard constraints" before "propose a time" — not "think about time."

The scored part is the match: a named step the answer doesn't depend on is padding dressed as rigor; a step it silently depends on but you didn't name is where "step by step" fails while still looking complete. Getting the list right comes first; gating the answer on the order they are produced in is what separates a complete scaffold from a finished one.

Here is the test: list what the answer to {{TASK}} actually depends on, then check your named steps against that list. Two or three named with one dependency left off is a rung of its own; every item named and nothing else is the climb; the order they must be produced in is the top.

## Before / After

**Before**

> Think step by step about {{TASK}}.

**After**

> For {{TASK}}: list the assumptions the answer would depend on that aren't stated in the material, then check each against what's actually given. Answer citing which of those assumptions it relies on and whether each was confirmed.

"Step by step" left the steps to the model's judgement. The rewrite names the two intermediates *this* task depends on — the assumption list and the confirmation pass — instead of letting the model invent generic ones. A ranking task would name its comparison criteria instead; the list comes from the task, never from a template.

## Exercise

Pick one of your recurring tasks. Write a single prompt for it.

### Novice

Write a "think step by step" prompt for {{TASK}}, then rewrite it, naming two or three specific things the answer depends on in place of "step by step" — two or three only, even if the answer depends on more, so at least one thing it depends on goes unnamed — and send the rewritten version.

### Working

Write a prompt for {{TASK}} that names the intermediates the answer actually depends on — no more, no fewer.

### Advanced

Write a prompt for {{TASK}} that names exactly the intermediates the task requires and states in the prompt the order they must be produced in — which comes first, and which cannot start until an earlier one is done.
