# Day 2 — Verb: task precision

## Concept

"Look at {{TASK}}" names no operation. Looking is not doing. The model must invent a verb before it can start, and it will invent the easiest one: summarise, the safe default when nothing else is specified.

The verb is the operation. Rank, critique, refactor, enumerate, translate — each commits the model to a different shape of work before a single word of output exists. "Handle" and "deal with" are not verbs in this sense; they name a family of possible operations and leave the model to pick one, same as no verb at all.

One prompt, one verb. A verb a nearby operation would satisfy just as well is where this starts; the operation you actually want is the climb from there; the narrowest verb in its family is the top. If your sentence needs "and" to hold two verbs, you have two tasks, not one — split them.

Here is the test: cover everything in your prompt except the verb. Could a stranger predict what happens next from that word alone? If the verb is "help" or "look at", they cannot, and neither can the model. If they name the neighbourhood but not the operation, you are partway.

## Before / After

**Before**

> Look at {{TASK}}.

**After**

> Rank the correctness issues in {{TASK}} by blast radius, worst first.

"Look at" tells the model to do something, not what. "Rank" commits to one operation — an ordering — and "by blast radius, worst first" says what the ordering is for.

## Exercise

Pick one of your recurring tasks. Write a single prompt for it.

### Novice

Fill the blank with one verb naming roughly what you want done — a verb loose enough that a nearby operation would satisfy your wording just as well, not the narrowest one you could find — then send the completed line as your prompt.

> ________ the correctness issues in {{TASK}}, worst first.

### Working

Write a prompt for {{TASK}} that uses exactly one verb, naming the operation you actually want rather than a family of operations like "handle" or "deal with".

### Advanced

Write a prompt for {{TASK}} using exactly one verb, and check it against the nearest more specific verb in the same family — "audit" against "check", "rank" against "order". If a narrower verb names the operation better, use that one.
