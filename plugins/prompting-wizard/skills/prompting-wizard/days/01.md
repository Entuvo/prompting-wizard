# Day 1 — Noun: name the artifact

## Concept

Most weak prompts never say what they want made. "Help me prepare for the parent meeting" names no artifact, so the model guesses: an agenda, a progress summary, a message to families, a question back. Any of those satisfies "help".

The noun is the deliverable. Naming it collapses the space of acceptable answers faster than any other lever. "A meeting agenda" and "a message inviting families" are both help; only one of them is what you wanted.

Here is the test: read your prompt and ask what physical thing lands when it finishes. If you cannot name it in a noun phrase, the model cannot either — so it will pick for you.

Nouns compound with counts and formats, but those are later levers. Today, just name the thing — and the top rung adds economy: no words spent on the artifact beyond what pins it down.

## Before / After

This fixed example illustrates the technique; apply it to your own task in the exercise.

**Before**

> Help me with the parent meeting.

**After**

> Help me with an agenda for the parent meeting.

The verb did not move. The noun did the work: "an agenda" is a thing that either exists at the end or does not, while count, format, and quality stay open for later lessons.

## Exercise

Pick one of your recurring tasks: a lesson outline, a staff handover, a customer reply, or another task you know well. Write a single prompt for it.

### Novice

Fill the blank with a noun phrase naming what you want back and what it covers — but not the count or the format, so a reader could still deliver two different things — then send the completed line as your prompt.

> Produce ________ for {{TASK}}.

### Working

Write a prompt for {{TASK}} whose deliverable is unambiguous. Someone reading only your prompt, without seeing any output, should be able to describe the finished artifact.

### Advanced

Write a prompt for {{TASK}} that names the artifact unambiguously in under 15 words total. Precision without length is the constraint — every word you spend has to be pinning the artifact down.
