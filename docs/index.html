Learn from the work you just did.

When you finish a task, the coach can offer one small lesson from it. You can stop after any step.

  • Practice

    Write your own change and predict its effect, then see what changed.

  • Example

    See a brief demonstration on your task.

  • Lesson

    Learn the principle using your task, then practise if you want.

  • Later

    Continue your work, with no penalty or backlog.

No improvement is a valid result. A short prompt can be enough.

See where your past prompts cost you turns.

On request, it looks back at your recent work for the follow-up that says “no, I meant…”, an interrupted run, a prompt you edited, or a rule you had to repeat.

  • A casebook of your own prompts, each with one edit that would have helped, and lesson suggestions.
  • It asks twice: before reading, and again before scoring.
  • Your history stays with its provider. Claude reviews Claude exports; ChatGPT and Codex review ChatGPT exports.
  • In Claude Code or Codex, results stay in a local folder you choose and expire automatically.

Carry your learning between chats.

A Learning Card, LEARNING.md, records what you practised and what you used on your own.

  • You keep it. Save it and attach it in your next chat, in ChatGPT or Claude.
  • Nothing syncs on its own. Without the current card, the coach can't know your learning history.
  • Help and independent use stay apart. Reading an example doesn't count as learning the skill.

The optional course: how a lesson works

For a structured path: an assessment, then thirty guided lessons, each built on a task from your own working life. Everyday lessons don't advance the course.

  • Write

    Your prompt for a real task.

  • Run

    It runs exactly as written; it is not quietly improved first.

  • Compare

    Against one targeted change, on the same material.

  • Improve

    Name the change that mattered and get a score.

Thirty lessons, four stages

  • Diagnose

    A short assessment records your starting level and the tasks you want help with.

  • Control

    Eleven language levers: the ordinary parts of language that control what gets made, in what scope, and to what standard.

  • Build

    Examples, output formats, multi-step tasks, self-review, evaluation criteria and reusable templates.

  • Harden

    A capstone prompt for one of your real tasks, tested on a case it wasn't designed for.

Who it's for

Anyone who already asks AI for help. No coding knowledge is required.

  • A teacher turns source material into a lesson plan with a defined age level and objective.
  • A lawyer extracts obligations, dates and exceptions, keeping conclusions tied to the text.
  • A manager turns meeting notes into decisions, owners and deadlines.
  • A researcher separates evidence from inference.

You bring the professional judgment.

Install one plugin to start.

What you need

  • Claude, ChatGPT, Claude Code or Codex. Prompting Wizard is one plugin for all four. Plugin uploads in the Claude app need a paid plan; in ChatGPT, a local plugin is shared through a workspace admin.
  • A task from your own work. The coach and the lessons use it, so keep one in mind.
  • A folder to practise in. See the note below the prompt.

Download for your app

  • Claude (web, desktop, Cowork, Claude Code): prompting-wizard-claude.zip. Upload it in Customize → Plugins; it also appears in Claude Code when you sign in with the same login.
  • ChatGPT and Codex: prompting-wizard-openai.zip. Register it as a local plugin in the ChatGPT desktop app, then a workspace admin can publish it; in Codex you can also add the marketplace below.
