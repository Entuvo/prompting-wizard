# Day 25 — Writing evals: write criteria before you look

## Concept

"Make sure it is good" names a feeling, not a test. Two people checking the same output against "good" will disagree, because nothing was written down for them to agree on — the standard lives in your head and shifts depending on the output in front of you.

Writing criteria first breaks that dependency. Three written checks — every claim has a source, no sentence over 25 words, the recommendation appears in the first paragraph — describe the output before it exists, so they can't bend to fit whatever the model happened to produce. Score against them afterwards, and two people applying the same three checks land on the same score.

The order is the whole discipline. Criteria written after the output are a justification for a verdict you already reached by feel. Written first, they're the test the output has to pass, and you don't get to relax one because this attempt came close.

Here is the test: show your criteria to someone who hasn't seen the output. Could they score it without asking you what you meant?

## Before / After

**Before**

> Make sure it is good.

**After**

> Before generating: {{TASK}} will be judged against three checks — every claim traces to a source, no paragraph exceeds five sentences, and the conclusion appears in the first paragraph. Now produce {{TASK}} and score it against those three checks, unchanged.

"Good" gave two scorers nothing to agree on. The three checks give them the same three things to look for, written down before the output existed to influence them.

## Exercise

Pick one of your recurring tasks. Write a single prompt for it.

### Novice

Before writing a prompt for {{TASK}}, write three checks you'll apply to whatever it produces. Only then write the prompt and generate the output.

### Working

Write three criteria for {{TASK}} specific enough that someone else could apply them without asking you what you meant, then generate the output and score it against them unchanged.

### Advanced

Write three criteria for {{TASK}} before generating anything, each specific enough that two readers who have not seen the output would produce the same score. After you see the output, score it against exactly those three — don't add a fourth, and don't soften one it narrowly misses.
