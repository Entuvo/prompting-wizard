# Day 2 — Verb: task precision

## Concept

"Look at {{TASK}}" names no operation. Looking is not doing. The model has to invent a verb before it can start, and it will invent the easiest one: summarise, probably, because summarising is the safe default when nothing else is specified.

The verb is the operation. Rank, critique, refactor, enumerate, translate — each commits the model to a different shape of work before a single word of output exists. "Handle" and "deal with" are not verbs in this sense; they name a family of possible operations and leave the model to pick a member of the family, same as no verb at all.

One prompt, one verb, doing the operation you actually want. If your sentence needs "and" to hold two verbs, you have two tasks, not one — split them.

Here is the test. Cover everything in your prompt except the verb. Could a stranger predict what happens next from that word alone? If the verb is "help" or "look at" or "consider", they cannot, and neither can the model.

## Before / After

**Before**

> Look at {{TASK}}.

**After**

> Rank the correctness issues in {{TASK}} by blast radius, worst first.

"Look at" tells the model to do something, not what. "Rank" commits to one operation — an ordering — and "by blast radius, worst first" says what the ordering is for.

## Exercise

Pick one of your recurring tasks. Write a single prompt for it.

### Novice

Fill the blank with one verb naming the exact operation you want performed, then send the completed line as your prompt.

> ________ the correctness issues in {{TASK}}, worst first.

### Working

Write a prompt for {{TASK}} that uses exactly one verb, and make it the verb that names the operation you actually want — not a generic stand-in like "check" or "handle".

### Advanced

Write a prompt for {{TASK}} using exactly one verb in the whole prompt. Every other word can name the artifact, the criteria, or the scope, but only one word may be doing.
