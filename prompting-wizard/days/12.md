# Day 12 — Interjection: attention markers

## Concept

Five instructions in a row read as five equal priorities, even when one matters far more than the rest. A must-not-fail instruction sitting third from last, unmarked, between a style note and a summary request, carries no more weight than either neighbour. Working through in order, a model can satisfy four well and quietly drop the fifth — it looked least important, because nothing said otherwise.

An interjection like "IMPORTANT:" breaks that equality, telling the reader — human or model — that this instruction isn't like its neighbours. But marking alone isn't enough: buried mid-paragraph, it still competes for attention. Standing alone, the same sentence becomes the hardest thing to have missed.

The marker is only honest if rare. If everything is IMPORTANT, nothing is. Marked inline among its neighbours is where this starts; standing alone but competing with one other marked item is the climb from there; exactly one marker, on the instruction whose failure you'd actually be angry about, is what makes the word mean something.

Here is the test: count the markers in your prompt, and ask whether the one you'd be angriest to see ignored stands alone on its own line.

## Before / After

**Before**

> Review {{TASK}} for correctness and style. Note any performance issues you notice along the way. Suggest a better structure if one is obvious. Do not suggest changing the public API — it's frozen for this release. Summarise your findings in five bullets at the end.

**After**

> Review {{TASK}} for correctness and style. Note any performance issues you notice along the way. Suggest a better structure if one is obvious. Summarise your findings in five bullets at the end. IMPORTANT: do not suggest changing the public API — it's frozen for this release.

The instruction didn't change; its weight did. Buried third from last and unmarked, it read as one of five equal notes. Marked and moved to stand alone at the end, it reads as the one thing that cannot slip.

## Exercise

Pick one of your recurring tasks. Write a single prompt for it.

### Novice

### Working

### Advanced
