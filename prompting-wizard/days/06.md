# Day 6 — Composing the first five

## Concept

You've practised noun, verb, adjective, adverb, and preposition one at a time. Nothing today is new. Today is about running them together, because each lever constrains a different dimension of the same request, and setting one does not substitute for setting the others.

The noun says what lands. The verb says what operation produces it. The adjective says what would make you reject it. The adverb says how thoroughly. The preposition says where it stops and who it's for. Naming the artifact tells the model nothing about depth; setting depth tells it nothing about audience. A sharp noun alone is exactly as underspecified on manner as no noun at all — just underspecified on a different axis.

A composed prompt is not five sentences bolted together. It's one sentence carrying five jobs, each done by one word or phrase, none fighting for the same space. That's what makes it short and still complete.

Test it by removing each lever in turn. If the sentence still reads as a full instruction with one gap, that lever wasn't pulling weight. If it collapses into ambiguity, it was.

## Before / After

**Before**

> Look at {{TASK}}.

**After**

> Rank the blunt, jargon-free correctness issues in {{TASK}} exhaustively, for a reader new to the codebase.

Every word above is doing exactly one lever's job: "rank" is the verb, "correctness issues" the noun, "blunt, jargon-free" the adjectives, "exhaustively" the adverb, "for a reader new to the codebase" the preposition. Nothing is left for the model to invent.
