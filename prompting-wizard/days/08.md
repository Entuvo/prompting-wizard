# Day 8 — Pronoun: reference binding

## Concept

"Look at this and fix it." Two pronouns, zero antecedents. "This" could be the diff, the file, or the codebase; "it" could be the bug, the file, or the PR. The model guesses what each points to, and picks whichever reading lets it start typing fastest — not the one you meant.

A pronoun is a shortcut for a noun already named — it only works once naming happens first. "Read the diff below. Fix the null-handling bug it introduces in the payment handler" has one pronoun, "it", and it resolves inside the same sentence, to "the null-handling bug".

Ambiguity survives even when an antecedent exists: if two nouns are candidates and either reading works, the pronoun is still broken. The reader — human or model — picks one silently, and you never find out it picked wrong.

Here is the test: for each it/this/that/these in your prompt, point at the exact word or quoted block it refers to. One you can't point at, with the rest resolving, is partway. Replacing that last one is the climb; putting each antecedent next to its pronoun is the rung above.

## Before / After

**Before**

> Look at this and fix it.

**After**

> Read the diff below. Fix the null-handling bug it introduces in the payment handler.

"This" is gone — the sentence names the diff outright. "It" resolves to that diff, the only candidate in the prompt, and no reader has to look further back than the previous sentence to find it.

## Exercise

Pick one of your recurring tasks. Write a single prompt for it.

### Novice

Take a prompt for {{TASK}} that uses at least two pronouns with no antecedent, then rewrite it by replacing all but one of them with the exact noun phrase each should have meant — leaving exactly one pronoun that still requires the reader to guess — and send the rewritten prompt.

### Working

Write a prompt for {{TASK}} where every pronoun has exactly one possible antecedent inside the prompt or in a block you quote, even if the reader has to look back a sentence or two to find it.

### Advanced

Write a prompt for {{TASK}} where each pronoun's antecedent is the nearest preceding noun phrase, so no reader has to look back past the previous clause. Keep the pronouns — replacing them with nouns is not the exercise.
