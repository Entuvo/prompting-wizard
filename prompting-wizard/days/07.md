# Day 7 — Review: rewrite your worst prompt

## Concept

Five levers in, the test isn't whether you can recognise a missing noun or a bare verb in someone else's example — it's whether you can find the gaps in a prompt you actually wrote and were unhappy with. That's a different skill. In your own writing, the missing pieces feel obvious to you, because you're reading your intent into the sentence, not the sentence itself. The model only ever gets the sentence.

Today has no seed prompt. You bring one: something you sent recently that came back wrong, vague, or not what you meant. The job is to work out which of the five levers — noun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition — was left open, and rewrite until none of them are.

A composed prompt earns its length. If you can delete a clause and the output wouldn't change, that clause wasn't constraining anything — it was decoration wearing a lever's clothes. The rewrite you're aiming for is the opposite: every clause, if removed, visibly weakens the result.

## Before / After

**Before**

The tutor asks the learner for a real prompt they sent recently and were unhappy with — one that produced a vague, wrong, or unusable result. That prompt, exactly as the learner wrote it, is the "before". Do not substitute an example; use the learner's own words.

**After**

The tutor does not supply a rewrite yet. The learner attempts their own rewrite first, addressing whichever of the five levers they judge were left open. Only after that attempt exists does the tutor produce a stronger version for comparison, scored the same way as the learner's attempt.

## Exercise

Bring a real prompt of yours that you were unhappy with — not {{TASK}} restated, an actual prompt you sent and got a weak result from.

### Novice

State which one of the five levers — noun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition — was most clearly missing from your original prompt, then rewrite only that lever in.

### Working

Rewrite your prompt so that all five levers are set, checking each one explicitly against the original before you send the new version.

### Advanced

Rewrite your prompt so that removing any single clause measurably degrades the output — no clause is decorative, every one is load-bearing. The artifact phrase and the quality words are where this bites hardest: neither may carry a word that isn't pinning something down.
