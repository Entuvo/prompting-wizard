# Day 13 — Particle: phrasal precision

## Concept

"Look over {{TASK}}", "look up {{TASK}}", and "look into {{TASK}}" share a verb and differ by one short word doing all the work: "over" means skim for anything obviously wrong; "up" means retrieve a fact; "into" means investigate a cause. Say "look over" when you meant "look up" and you get a skim where you wanted a lookup — wrong operation, nothing looked broken.

The particle is the small word stuck to a verb that changes what it means: check in / check out, look up / look over, work through / work around. Swap it and the operation changes, often completely, while the sentence still reads fine — that's what makes a careless particle dangerous. "Look over each external call" doesn't signal a check against documentation; "look up" does.

A particle earns its place only if changing it would change the task. If two particles satisfy your prompt equally well, neither is load-bearing — decoration wearing a verb's clothes.

Here is the test: swap the particle for a plausible alternative. If the task changed, keep it. If it didn't, use a plain verb instead.

## Before / After

**Before**

> Look over {{TASK}}.

**After**

> Look up each external call in {{TASK}} and check it against its documented contract.

"Over" means skim; "up" means retrieve and compare. Swapping one word turned a glance into a check against documentation — same sentence shape, a different operation.

## Exercise

Pick one of your recurring tasks. Write a single prompt for it.

### Novice

Fill the blank with the phrasal verb you'd reach for by habit rather than one whose particle you've chosen deliberately — close to right, with a small ambiguity a stricter synonym would remove — then send the completed line as your prompt.

> ________ each external call in {{TASK}} and check it against its documented contract.

### Working

Write a prompt for {{TASK}} using one phrasal verb, choosing its particle deliberately rather than by habit: swapping the particle for a plausible alternative should visibly change the task.

### Advanced

Write a prompt for {{TASK}} using one phrasal verb, then test it twice: swap the particle for a plausible alternative, and replace the whole phrasal verb with a plain verb. Keep the phrasal verb only if both swaps visibly change the task; if the plain verb serves just as well, use the plain verb instead.
