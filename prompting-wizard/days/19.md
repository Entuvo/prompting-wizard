# Day 19 — Reasoning scaffolds: name the intermediates

## Concept

"Think step by step about {{TASK}}" asks for reasoning without saying what the steps are. The model produces steps — some invented, in whatever order looks plausible — and you can't tell whether the ones that mattered actually appeared, because you never named them.

A scaffold names the specific intermediates the task depends on, not generic "steps." For a scheduling conflict, that's "list each commitment's hard constraints" before "propose a time" — not "think about time." The steps come from what the task needs, not from what makes reasoning look thorough.

The hard part, and the scored part, is the match: a named step the answer doesn't depend on is padding dressed as rigor; a step it silently depends on but you didn't name is where "step by step" fails while still looking complete. Naming steps can also gate the answer on them — useful, but secondary to getting the list right.

Here is the test: list what the answer to {{TASK}} actually depends on, then check your named steps against that list. Anything named that isn't on it, cut. Anything on the list that isn't named, add.

## Before / After

**Before**

> Think step by step about {{TASK}}.

**After**

> For {{TASK}}: list the assumptions the answer would depend on that aren't stated in the material, then check each against what's actually given. Answer citing which of those assumptions it relies on and whether each was confirmed.

"Step by step" left the steps to the model's judgement. The rewrite names the two intermediates the answer actually depends on — the assumption list and the confirmation pass — instead of letting the model invent generic ones.

## Exercise

Pick one of your recurring tasks. Write a single prompt for it.

### Novice

Write a "think step by step" prompt for {{TASK}}, then rewrite it, replacing "step by step" with the specific things the answer actually depends on.

### Working

Write a prompt for {{TASK}} that names the intermediates the answer actually depends on — no more, no fewer — so a reader could tell you removed a step because the task doesn't need it, not because you ran out of space.

### Advanced

Write a prompt for {{TASK}} that names exactly the intermediates the task requires. For each one you named, be ready to say what breaks in the answer if it's cut; for each one you left out, be ready to say why the task doesn't depend on it.
