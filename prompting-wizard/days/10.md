# Day 10 — Determiner: definiteness and quantity

## Concept

"Fix issues in {{TASK}}" leaves "issues" bare. A bare noun doesn't say how many: one issue, a handful, or all of them? "The issues" implies a set already agreed on; "any issue" implies fixing whichever one you find first and stopping. Each reading produces different work, and the model picks one without telling you which.

A determiner binds a noun to a quantity and definiteness: the (a known set), a (one, unspecified), each (one at a time, all of them), every (all of them, together), any (one, arbitrarily chosen). "Fix every correctness issue in {{TASK}}. Leave each style issue alone" tells the model exactly how much of each category to touch.

The nouns that matter most are the ones where swapping the determiner changes scope: "the bug" versus "a bug", "every reviewer" versus "any reviewer". A bare noun next to a bound one is still a gap.

Here is the test: put the/a/each/every/any in front of every noun in your prompt and ask which swap would change what gets done. If a noun has none, that's the gap.

## Before / After

**Before**

> Fix issues in {{TASK}}.

**After**

> Fix every correctness issue in {{TASK}}. Leave each style issue alone.

"Every" commits to the full set of correctness issues; "each" forces style issues to be visited individually and left exactly as found. Neither noun is left bare for the model to scope on its own.
