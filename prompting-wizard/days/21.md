# Day 21 — Context ordering: task, material, constraints

## Concept

Three hundred words of context before the instruction means the model reads the whole block without knowing what it's for, then has to look back to work out which parts mattered. Everything gets equal attention on the first pass, because nothing has said yet what to look for.

Reordering the same content — task first, material second, constraints last — changes nothing about what's included, only when it's read. Task first gives every sentence that follows something to be filtered against. Material second supplies what needs filtering. Constraints last land right before the model starts writing, instead of getting skimmed a paragraph ago and forgotten by the end.

This is a pure reorder, not a trim. The test is whether you deleted anything to make the new version read better — if you did, the old order wasn't hiding structure, it was hiding padding you should have cut anyway.

Here is the test: before you run the reordered version, predict what changes about the output. If nothing does, either order didn't matter here, or you didn't reorder enough to actually test it.

## Before / After

**Before**

> This project has been through three previous reviewers, two of whom left before finishing and one of whom left comments that were never resolved. The current draft incorporates feedback from a stakeholder meeting six weeks ago, where the scope was narrowed twice — once to cut a feature nobody could agree on, and once because the original timeline slipped by a month. There is a related piece of earlier work that took a different approach and was later abandoned; it's worth knowing about because some of the same names and examples resurface in the current draft, and a careless reader could think they're the same effort. The style guide changed halfway through writing, so earlier sections follow different conventions than later ones, and nobody has gone back to unify them. Two people on the team disagree about whether the tone should be formal or conversational, and that disagreement is visible if you read closely — the opening reads one way, the closing another. The intended audience is a mix of technical and non-technical readers, which nobody has fully reconciled either. Nothing can reference the abandoned prior approach by name, for legal reasons. The finished piece also has to fit in a single email without attachments. Review {{TASK}} and tell me what to fix before it goes out.

> Constraints: nothing can reference the abandoned prior approach by name, for legal reasons. The finished piece also has to fit in a single email without attachments.

Same words, same count, moved. The task now filters every sentence that follows it — a reviewer knows from line one that the mismatched tone and unreconciled audience are fix candidates, not backstory. The two constraints, grouped at the end instead of buried mid-paragraph, are the last thing read before the model starts writing.

## Exercise

Before presenting a tier, read `## Levers` from the learner's `PROGRESS.md`, identify the three lowest-scoring levers, and name them to the learner. Build the context-heavy material below around exercising those three — for example, if pronoun is weak, thread an unresolved "it" through the paragraph; if numeral is weak, bury a quantity in prose instead of stating it as a number; if conjunction is weak, hide an unstated fallback inside a sentence.

### Novice

Ask the learner for a real task from their `## Tasks` entries. Have them write 200+ words of context and material for it with the instruction buried at the end, unreordered. Read it back and point out where the instruction sits.

### Working

Ask the learner to reorder their prompt into task, material, constraints — without deleting a word — and to predict, before running it, what changes about the output. Check the prediction against the real run.

### Advanced

Ask the learner to reorder a prompt for a real task into task, material, constraints, predicting the change first, while also fixing the three levers named as weakest wherever they surface in the reordered material.
